### **POI 180: Ancient Theatre of Thorikos**
**Location**: Lavrio, Southeastern Attica  
**Coordinates**: 37.738°N, 24.054°E  
**Significance**: **World's oldest known theater** (525-480 BC)

**Description**: The unique elongated stone theater at Thorikos is one of the earliest surviving theaters in the world. Unlike typical semicircular Greek theaters, it has an unusual oval shape with a rectangular orchestra. Built during the Archaic period, it predates the standardized theater form and offers insights into early theatrical architecture. The site includes 21 rows with capacity for 4,000 spectators, plus ruins of a small temple and altar to Dionysus.

**Why Add**: Historic significance as world's oldest theater, unique architecture, close to Lavrio silver mines

### **POI 181: Mycenaean Tholos Tombs of Thorikos**
**Location**: Velatouri Hill, Thorikos, Lavrio  
**Coordinates**: 37.740°N, 24.055°E  
**Significance**: Monumental Bronze Age burial structures

**Description**: Two impressive Mycenaean tholos tombs on Velatouri Hill date back to the 2nd millennium BCE (c. 1600-1200 BC). These beehive-shaped tombs demonstrate the region's prehistoric importance and elite burial practices. Tomb III is a true tholos, while Tomb IV is elliptical. The tombs illustrate the evolution of funerary architecture and testify to Thorikos' role as a major center due to silver mining since the Neolithic period.

**Why Add**: Prehistoric significance, connects to ancient mining history, complements theater site

### **POI 182: Sanctuary of Artemis Brauronia (Vravrona)**
**Location**: Vravrona (Brauron), Eastern Attica  
**Coordinates**: 38.003°N, 23.993°E  
**Significance**: Major sanctuary dedicated to Artemis, goddess of childbirth

**Description**: One of the most important sanctuaries in ancient Attica, dedicated to Artemis as protector of women and childbirth. The site features a Doric temple (c. 500 BC), a unique π-shaped stoa with dining rooms, a sacred spring, cave shrines, and the tomb of Iphigenia. Famous for the Arkteia festival where young girls (arktoi - "little bears") aged 5-10 performed ritual dances. The sanctuary was active from the 8th century BC until the 3rd century BC when it was destroyed by flooding.

**Why Add**: Major religious site, unique female-centered cult, excellent museum nearby

### **POI 183: Sacred Spring of Brauron**
**Location**: Sanctuary of Artemis, Vravrona  
**Coordinates**: 38.003°N, 23.993°E  
**Significance**: Most sacred part of the Brauron sanctuary

**Description**: A copious natural spring at the heart of the Brauron sanctuary, considered the most sacred spot until the late 6th century BC. Archaeological excavations recovered numerous offerings thrown into the waters - mirrors, rings, gems, bone and wood objects (preserved in mud), mostly of feminine character. Women who had successful childbirths offered clothes and personal items here, while those who died in childbirth had their garments offered at Iphigenia's tomb.

**Why Add**: Unique ritual site, exceptional archaeological preservation, complements main sanctuary

### **POI 184: Sanctuary of Athena Pallenis**
**Location**: Pallini (ancient Pallene), Mesogeia Plain  
**Coordinates**: 38.003°N, 23.893°E  
**Significance**: Rural sanctuary and center of ancient federation

**Description**: One of the most famous rural shrines of ancient Attica. The mid-5th century BC Doric hexastyle temple was designed by the "architect of Theseion" (same as Temple of Hephaestus, Poseidon at Sounion, and Nemesis at Rhamnous). The sanctuary served as the center of a federation that included Pallene, Gargettos, Paiania, and probably Acharnae. The area is connected to myths of the Gigantomachy, Heracleids, and Theseus. Many marble temple elements were reused in Christian churches.

**Why Add**: Important regional sanctuary, architectural significance, mythological connections

### **POI 185: Ancient Deme of Sphettos**
**Location**: Near Koropi (Kastro tou Christou hill), Mesogeia Plain  
**Coordinates**: 37.900°N, 23.887°E  
**Significance**: One of the original twelve townships that formed Attica under Theseus

**Description**: Sphettos was one of the ancient twelve townships united by Theseus to form Attica. The deme center was located on a prominent hill crowned with a monastery about 3 km west of modern Koropi. Archaeological evidence includes an inscribed base for a statue of Demetrios of Phaleron dedicated by the demesmen, cuttings for votive reliefs, terracing from a small rural sanctuary, and numerous inscriptions built into houses and churches. Two small Classical temples on Mount Hymettus overlooked the settlement.

**Why Add**: Historic political significance, connects to Theseus legend, Mesogeia region representation

### **POI 186: Ancient Silver Mines of Laurion**
**Location**: Lavreotiki region, southeastern Attica  
**Coordinates**: 37.710°N, 24.020°E  
**Significance**: Funded Athens' golden age and naval power

**Description**: The extensive ancient silver mining complex at Laurion was crucial to Athens' wealth and power in the Classical period. Mining began in the Final Neolithic/Early Bronze Age (c. 3200 BC) and continued for millennia. The silver extracted funded the construction of the Parthenon, Athens' fleet that defeated Persia at Salamis, and much of the city's golden age infrastructure. The landscape is dotted with ancient mine shafts, ore washeries, cisterns, towers, and smelting furnaces demonstrating remarkable ancient engineering and metallurgy.

**Why Add**: Economic foundation of Classical Athens, industrial archaeology, extensive visible remains

---

### **POI 187: Ancient Ore Washeries of Souriza Valley**
**Location**: Souriza-Agrileza valley, Lavreotiki  
**Coordinates**: 37.715°N, 24.015°E  
**Significance**: Best-preserved ancient ore processing facilities in Greece

**Description**: Scattered throughout the Souriza valley are remarkably well-preserved ancient ore washeries and processing installations from the Classical and Hellenistic periods. These stone structures used sophisticated hydraulic systems to separate silver from lead ore. The washeries demonstrate the high technological level of ancient Athenian mining operations. Marked hiking trails through the valley allow visitors to explore multiple installations while enjoying the Mediterranean landscape.

**Why Add**: Industrial archaeology, accessible hiking trails, complements mines POI

---

### **POI 188: Fortified Acropolis of Thorikos**
**Location**: Velatouri Hill summit, Thorikos  
**Coordinates**: 37.742°N, 24.056°E  
**Significance**: Prehistoric settlement and defensive position

**Description**: The fortified acropolis atop Velatouri Hill shows continuous habitation from the Neolithic through the Late Bronze Age (c. 3200-1050 BC), with particular flourishing during 2000-1600 BC. The defensible hilltop location, natural harbor at Agios Nikolaos below, and rich silver-bearing bedrock made this an ideal settlement. Archaeological excavations revealed houses, graves, roads, shrines, and industrial installations. The commanding position offers panoramic views of the Aegean Sea and surrounding mining landscape.

**Why Add**: Prehistoric settlement, strategic location, completes Thorikos archaeological complex

---

### **POI 189: Temple of Demeter at Thorikos (Adami Plain Stoa)**
**Location**: Adami Plain, Thorikos  
**Coordinates**: 37.735°N, 24.050°E  
**Significance**: Monumental Doric stoa, possibly part of Demeter sanctuary

**Description**: In the Adami plain near Thorikos stands a monumental Doric stoa, likely part of a sanctuary dedicated to Demeter, goddess of agriculture and the harvest. Built during the Classical period's floruit (5th-4th centuries BC) when Thorikos became a multifaceted town, the structure reflects the prosperity brought by silver mining. The sanctuary would have served both religious and social functions for the mining community, providing a gathering place for festival celebrations and agricultural rites.

**Why Add**: Agricultural/religious significance, completes Thorikos complex, represents daily life
